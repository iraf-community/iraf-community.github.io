The answer to this question may be that its not supported but I'm <br>
puzzled as to what I found recently when I tried to build IRAF under <br>
Centos 4.1<br>
<br>
   This is what I found. IRAF built and installed with no problems at <br>
all. I then started to build some packages I needed. Fitsutil and color <br>
were again no problems. When I came to gmisc (and I had similar problems <br>
with nmisc) I got messages in the spool file that dcgms_ and some other <br>
routines were being called with inconsistent numbers of parameters when <br>
compiling t_dispcor.x  Sure enough, if I looked at the source of <br>
t_dispcor.x there were places where the routine was being called with 6 <br>
parameters and places where it was being called with 8. From the <br>
declaration I would have expected it to have 8.<br>
<br>
  Previously I had built these packages under Red Hat 9.0 with no <br>
problems so I ran xc with the -F switch and compared the two .f files <br>
generated on the two OS's. The fortran generated was identical on the <br>
two. I then did a bit of digging around and worked out that the f2c <br>
compiler seemed to be using the switches -ARw8 -Nn802. Since I had the <br>
t_dispcor.c file on Centos left over after the build had bombed out, I <br>
ran f2c on RH9.0 with the above switches and compared the two C code <br>
files. Once again they were identical and indeed dcgms_ was called in <br>
different places with different numbers of parameters.<br>
<br>
   The mystery to me is how the system built apparently correctly under <br>
RH9.0 rather than why it fails under Centos. It appears under Centos <br>
that the compiler is correctly pointing out that the routine is being <br>
called inconsistently.<br>
<br>
   Any pointers as to what is going on would be gratefully received.<br>

Hi Chris,<br>
	It appears that some of the newer GCC versions are a bit <br>
stricter about prototyping and creating errors where they used to<br>
be ignored.  I couldn't find a flag to disable this checking but<br>
you can work around it by using G77 as the compiler rather than <br>
the F2C/f77.sh script.  To do this, define<br>
<br>
	setenv XC_F77	g77<br>
<br>
Put this in your .cshrc file (or the equivalent if you use bash) so<br>
it will be picked up by the mkpkg/xc properly and it should allow<br>
the mismatched arguments.<br>
	We'll patch the code for future releases and this is only<br>
a temporary workaround.  For packages which still won't compile then<br>
using prebuilt binaries should also work (let us know if you can't <br>
find binaries for a particular package).  Hope this helps.<br>
