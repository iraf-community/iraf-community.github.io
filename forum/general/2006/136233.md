**David Thompson** wrote on Nov 08, 2006

{% raw %}
<div>
There is probably not a quick way to do this with an @list, but your problem is certainly solvable. <br>
<br>
It looks like you are taking a sequence of images from a camera not mounted to de-rotate the sky (equatorial mount or 3-axis alt-az-rot).  If so, then each of your images will be rotated by a fixed amount relative to the parallactic angle (which changes with time and position on the sky).  You will need an accurate time tag to calculate the parallactic angle.  <br>
<br>
If you simply want a relative rotation from one frame to the next, you can match any two stars in a pair of images (4 coords total) and calculate the relative rotation with simple trig.<br>
<br>
Your other option is to solve for the WCS on each image individually (see msccmatch task) and then use something like drizzle or SWarp to combine them.<br>
<br>
Dave - LBT Observatory
</div>
{% endraw %}

**Eugene Romas** wrote on Nov 08, 2006

{% raw %}
<div>
Ok. Now i use next:<br>
<pre><code>
imcoadd @m31_list asection=&quot;[770:1400,1000:1700] [1700:2100,1000:1500]&quot; alignmethod=twodx rotate+ scale+
</code></pre><br>
If software found too much stars, i use "gain" parameter with low than default value. For example:<br>
<pre><code>
imcoadd @m31_list asection=&quot;[770:1400,1000:1700] [1700:2100,1000:1500]&quot; alignmethod=twodx rotate+ scale+ gain=0.3
</code></pre><br>
About "too much stars": NORMAL value is near 300-700 stars. Too high value is &gt;1500.<br>
On my Celeron D 2.8GHz(up to 3.3GHz) with 1Gb RAM and SATA harddrive(AHCI mode), centering of 10 FITS files with 673 stars take a time near 10-12 minutes(with colebration by darks,flats and biases). But in the DeepSkyStacker under WinXP SP2 indentical task take near 1 hour!!!<br>
IRAF + Linux = ultra high speed!<br>
All what i wrote is "IMHO"...
