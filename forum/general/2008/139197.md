# Signal-to-noise ratio of a certain star?

---

**Philip Massey** wrote on Mar 20, 2008

{% raw %}
<div>
If you run imexamine on your fits image, put the cursor on your star, and<br>
hit the "r" key, you will see a bunch of numbers displayed.<br>
The third of these is the integrated number of counts of the star after<br>
background has been removed.  Let's call that F.<br>
The fourth is the average number of counts<br>
in the background.  Let's call that B.<br>
 These numbers will be meaningful as long as you have<br>
set reasonable values in "rimexam" for the object radius (R), background buffer<br>
width, and background width.<br>
<br>
Assuming that your "fits image" is a CCD frame for which you know the<br>
gain "g" [electrons/ADU] and read noise r [in electrons] then the<br>
"signal" will be the F*gain<br>
<br>
What will the noise be?  One component will be the photon statistics of the<br>
star itself:  sqrt(F*gain).  Another will be the photon noise from the sky:<br>
sqrt(N*B*gain), where N is the number of pixels in your aperture, i.e.,<br>
3.14159*R**2.<br>
Another will be due to the readout noise in your aperture, sqrt(N)*R.<br>
All of these sources of noise are independent, so you have to add them<br>
in quadrature, i.e.,<br>
noise = sqrt [ F*gain + N*B*gain + N*R**2]<br>
<br>
You might want to read through some photometry guides, such as the <br>
"How to do stellar photometry with IRAF" in the documentation section.
</div>
{% endraw %}
