The CRPIX values are the reference pixels for the <strong>WCS</strong> zero point (most likely the pixels corresponding to the nominal RA and Dec for the observation). WCS axes are generally not aligned with x and y but typically RA and Dec. The rotation between world co-ordinates and pixel rows/columns is specified by the keywords CD1_1 etc. CD1_1 is the variation of the first WCS co-ordinate with the first image axis, CD1_2 is the variation of the first WCS co-ordinate with the second image axis and so on. These keywords make up the elements of an affine transformation matrix, but normally there is no skew and the transformation is just a scaling and rotation. You can read more about the WCS representations here:<br>
<br>
 <a href="http://fits.gsfc.nasa.gov/fits_wcs.html">http://fits.gsfc.nasa.gov/fits_wcs.html</a><br>
<br>
Don't worry about the more complex representations discussed in those papers. If your CTYPE keywords are RA--TAN and DEC--TAN then the transformation is fairly straightforward.<br>
<br>
Cheers,<br>
<br>
James.

**Mike Fitzpatrick** wrote on Jan 21, 2009

{% raw %}
<div>
The thing to remember about FITS WCS is that the CRPIX keywords are the image coordinates corresponding to the CRVAL wcs coordinates, i.e. you can only use a difference in CRPIX as an initial shift if the CRVAL keywords are the same between the two images.  <br>
<br>
In general, RA and DEC keywords (and TELROT) come from the telescope control system and have no reliable meaning in the WCS, they could be the telescope pointing, the nominal object position, center of the FOV, etc.  CRVAL is the ra/dec at the CRPIX position in the image, the CD matrix represents the scale/rotation as James mentioned.  To quickly compute the scale and rotation you can use the equations<br>
<br>
<pre><code>
        scale = 3600. * sqrt ((cd11**2+cd21**2+cd12**2+cd22**2)/2.)
        xrot  = abs (datan ( cd21, cd11))
        yrot  = abs (datan (-cd12, cd22))
        rot   = (xrot + yrot) / 2.0
</code></pre><br>
<br>
This gives a scale in arcsec/pixel and rotation in degrees.  If you want to do this the long way you can use these values to determine a pixel shift given the CRVAL/CRPIX in each image.<br>
<br>
Another method is to use the SKYCTRAN task to compute the ra/dec for a known coordinate, and assuming the rotations are similar, the offset is a simple matter of scale.  For example,<br>
<br>
<pre><code>
print (&quot;1.0 1.0&quot;) | skyctran STDIN STDOUT &quot;imgname logical&quot; &quot;imgname world&quot;
</code></pre><br>
<br>
will print the coords of the lower-left corner of an image.  Do this for each image,  just compute the offset and divide by the scale (in degrees) to get the pixel shift.  You can use the central pixel just as easily.  Hope this helps.<br>
<br>
-Mike
</div>
