**Mike Fitzpatrick** wrote on Aug 13, 2009

{% raw %}
<div>
The FWHM of the correlation function is related to the average width of the spectral lines, and since you may have blended features or large lines like<br>
H-beta this could throw off the values greatly.  The velocity dispersion is a function of both the true dispersion and the instrumental width, by correlating stars with stars you can get an idea of the latter.  Accuracy also depends a bit on the line shapes, i.e. the convolution of a Gaussian with a Gaussian is a Gaussian, but if your spectral features have broad wings you may be better off fitting e.g. a Lorentzian functon rather than a Gaussian to get a more realistic width value.<br>
<br>
In any case, since the data are rebinned to a log-linear dispersion before correlation, the width can be multiplied directly by the velocity-per-pixel dispersion to get a value in km/s, but it's up to you to do a proper analysis to make sure the derived width is reasonable.  Hope this helps.<br>
<br>
-Mike
</div>
