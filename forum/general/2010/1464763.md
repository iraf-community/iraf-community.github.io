**Mike Fitzpatrick** wrote on Aug 12, 2010

{% raw %}
<div>
<blockquote><br>
1. I usually display more than 10 images in ds9 and I use button 'a' of imexa on them and log the report. I noticed that after frame 10 it goes back to 1, i.e. it does not read the value of the correspondent image, but it goes back to the first frame. I tried changing nframes in epar imexa but wth no success.</blockquote><br>
<br>
There are two issues here:  Are you running a sufficiently recent version of DS9 that it allows more than 4 frames (e.g. can you use DISPLAY from the CL to load frame 5 or do you get prompted that the max is 4)?  Second is whether or not DS9 is properly encoding the frame number.  Are you displaying initially from the CL or using DS9 directly and then running IMEXAM?  The frame number is a simple bitmask and a change to the code was required in order to allow more than 4 frames.  The wraparound with frame 10 seems to be an issue with how the frame is being interpreted.<br>
<br>
Can't you just run IMEXAM on a list of images rather than open the frames first?<br>
<br>
<blockquote><br>
2. Can more than 16 images be displayed from cl ? I can if I open them directly from ds9 but levels are different and it turns out more uncomfortable.</blockquote><br>
<br>
16 frames is the max allowed by the IIS protocol.  The frame designation is a simple 16-bit bitmask, so even though DS9 might have more frames open it may respond with a frame value other than the real value because of the way the bitmask is interpreted.<br>
<br>
[/quote]

**Mike Fitzpatrick** wrote on Aug 12, 2010

{% raw %}
<div>
 9 frames is a limitation (bug?) in DS9, you could use XImtool but then you'd still be limited to batches of 16.<br>
<br>
To process many images, a task like WREGISTER or IMSHIFT could be used to align the images, then call IMEXAM as e.g.<br>
<br>
<pre><code>cl&gt; imexam *.fits 1</code></pre><br>
<br>
All the images would be processed in frame 1 and you'd use the 'n' and 'p' (next and previous) keystrokes to move through the list.
</div>
