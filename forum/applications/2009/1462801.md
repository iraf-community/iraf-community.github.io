<div>
Hey<br>
<br>
I've done lots of photometry testing lately and when I started doing PSF fitting with DAOPHOT some of these bad analytic PSFs showed up. In my case it turned out, that it simply was a bad choice of PSF stars in the frame. I chose to let DAOPHOT decide which star to use, but this strongly depends on the completeness of your coordinate list, since all double stars would have to be listet. Its best to build your PSF interactively to see which stars give the best result and in the next step you can choose exactly these without doing interactive work.<br>
<br>
I tried to automate the process, because I have to do time series photometry in crowded fields. I managed to get some results with that by choosing only certain stars in the frame as PSF candidates, but still you will have ~1% of the total flux of a star as residuals after you subtract the analytical model...even with a good model (which is not good enough for high precision photometry); so I switched to a different program in combination with DAOPHOT. I now wonder how people get good PSF photometry from a several 1000 frames...<br>
<br>
Set all parameters right and choose good PSF stars interactively!<br>
The hint with trying it just on one star is also a good one, since you will be able to see if you are completely going down the wrong way.
</div>
