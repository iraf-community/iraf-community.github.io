Morning Frank, let me open by thanking you(!)  This forum has been especially useful.  Most often one stumbles upon the answers to their questions in old posts, so I am providing the information below for those users.  As a starting pint, here are some lists of spectral lines that are useful for spectral types B0-B9, see:<br>
<a href="http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1976PDAO...14..367B">http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1976PDAO...14..367B</a> <br>
<a href="http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1983A%26A...122...23A">http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1983A%26A...122...23A</a><br>
<br>
I also found the database of Kurucz to be useful: <a href="http://www.cfa.harvard.edu/amp/ampdata/kurucz23/sekur.html">http://www.cfa.harvard.edu/amp/ampdata/kurucz23/sekur.html</a>  <br>
<br>
Secondly, with regards to B-type spectra being available for the determination of radial velocities by means of cross-correlation and the testing of an adopted line list for RVIDLINES, I found the archive of Walborn &amp; Fitzpatrick ( <a href="http://cdsarc.u-strasbg.fr/viz-bin/VizieR-2?-source=III/195">http://cdsarc.u-strasbg.fr/viz-bin/VizieR-2?-source=III/195</a> ) to be useful as a starting point.  One can download flux-normalized spectra from that Vizier url.  Most often it is the case that folks use synthetic spectra for cross-correlation, or even better, standards of the same spectral type that they observed concurrent to their targets.<br>
<br>
CHEERS(!)
