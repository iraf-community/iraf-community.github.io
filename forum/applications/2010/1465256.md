<br>
Anyway, this post is about a bad combination of<br>
 mscred.ccdproc &amp; twod.longslit.response<br>
When reducing MEF-spectroscopy data I noticed that the<br>
mcsred/ccdrpoc didn't do "anything" to the image.<br>
Data: -internal flat (halogen lamp)<br>
     -science spectrum<br>
Data reduction:<br>
   *bias subtraction &amp; trim (all files)<br>
   *combining flats with mscred/flatcombine<br>
   *create a response image with twod.longslit/response<br>
<br>
I found that if I use<br>
  *noao.imred.ccdproc / imarith  (as image[1]) or mscred.mscarith<br>
   the image is reduced OK.<br>
     -However if I use mscred.ccdproc no correction was evident.<br>
<br>
I found from the iraf.net-posts that CCDMEAN has been a bug in<br>
mscred ( I can't remember the number, old one, solved by Frank Valdes),<br>
so I removed the CCDMEAN keyword from the<br>
(response) image header and after that mscred.ccdproc<br>
worked as expected.<br>
<br>
Is there any other way to "fix" this? I find mscred.ccdproc much more<br>
user-friendly for the MEF-files than the noao.imred.ccdproc, however it<br>
appears that I have to remember to remove the CCDMEAN-keyword<br>
from the "response"-image.<br>
<br>
By the way I'm using<br>
 "NOAO PC-IRAF Revision 2.12.2a-EXPORT Wed Jul 14 20:45:34 MST 2004"<br>
