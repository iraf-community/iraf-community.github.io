<div>
Hi Jim,.<br>
<br>
IRAF has been used in the past for medical imaging (e.g. cat brain slices at UCLA and mammography are two I remember) and while I'd heard of the 'Cellular Astronomy' paper before I couldn't say specifically that Shapiro ever used IRAF.  A similar site you should look at is <a href="http://astromed.iic.harvard.edu/">http://astromed.iic.harvard.edu/</a>.<br>
<br>
As for IRAF, I think what you'd be after are tasks in the photometry packages for detecting and measuring objects in an image (in your case, cells).  The DAOPHOT package has a task DAOFIND that can do this, likewise there is a more general STARFIND task that does something similar.  We also have a catalog-generation package called ACE, parts of which are in the system already (but hidden) but this is mostly still in development as an external package.  The counterpart the that is the SExtractor task (contact Emmanuel Bertin<br>
(bertin@iap.fr) or see <a href="http://terapix.iap.fr/rubrique.php?id_rubrique=91/)">http://terapix.iap.fr/rubrique.php?id_rubrique=91/)</a> while also produces a detection catalog.<br>
<br>
I'm afraid there's a limited amount we know that could help specifically with the blood-cell aspect, but please post any questions you have as well as any success in using IRAF in this way.<br>
<br>
Cheers,<br>
-Mike
</div>
